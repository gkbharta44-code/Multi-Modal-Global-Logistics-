1. Strategic Mandate & Vision

Modern global commerce is structurally bottlenecked by fragmented logistics networks, manual customs compliance, and aging physical infrastructure. Total global logistics spending currently commands between $11 trillion and $13 trillion annually, with up to $800 billion wasted entirely on administrative friction, port demurrage, and cross-border delays.

Project Iron Nexus is a coordinated, global sovereign initiative designed to integrate the world’s supply chains by combining industrialisation, supply chain management, and multimodal transport. It links deepwater ocean ports, dedicated rail corridors, express highways, and digital customs gateways under a single ledger. It is one of two projects in the $102.00 trillion programme, alongside Project Make in India ($56.00 trillion).

2. Capital Expenditure & Funding

Project Iron Nexus is funded with $46.00 trillion of bonds, which is 45.1% of the $102.00 trillion total outflow. Bharat bonds fund the remaining 54.9%. The Group summary states that the whole outflow is funded by bonds. Iron Nexus bonds carry a further $6.59 trillion of capitalised construction interest, which brings their balance to $52.59 trillion before repayments.

3. Debt Architecture & Repayment

Both bond series are 10-year bonds at 9% interest. Repayment comes from the shared net profit pool, Iron Nexus first and then Bharat. Track 1 (bulk) and Track 2 (retail) repayments reduce the Iron Nexus balance from Year 1. From Year 11, Aegis revenue also services the loans, per the Group summary.

4. Multimodal Debt Architecture & Credit Enhancement

To protect the project from high standalone sovereign risk, the coalition integrates a supranational credit enhancement structure. Development finance institutions can act as co-issuers, guarantors and financial catalysts rather than funding the full balance directly.

  • Guarantee engine: The World Bank Group and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) act as joint guarantors on construction tranches. The Group summary names these institutions but does not set out the guarantee terms.
  • Macroeconomic surveillance: The International Monetary Fund (IMF) runs debt sustainability analyses on participating nations during the build period.

Not in the Group summary, to be confirmed: the 4.75% to 5.50% yield range, the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA) cover, and the first-loss structure. The Group summary keeps 9% on both bond series.

Macroeconomic justification

The network removes structural economic waste that shippers, freight forwarders and carriers currently absorb. Their net cost of moving goods falls below historical benchmarks, which is why the toll is expected to be accepted.

6. Distributions to GK Bharta

GK Bharta receives no profit share until all loans are cleared. Distributions begin in Year 27, after both bond series are retired, and from then on GK Bharta receives 100% of net profit. The GK Bharta bond-purchase schedules in the earlier version, which assumed a 40% profit share from Year 15, are not in the Group summary and have been removed.
